How AOMedia Ensures AVIF is Patent-Free

The Alliance for Open Media (AOMedia) guarantees that the AV1 Image File Format (AVIF) remains patent-free and royalty-free through a comprehensive intellectual property framework. This article examines the core mechanisms AOMedia uses to safeguard AVIF, including strict member licensing agreements, defensive termination clauses, thorough patent analysis during development, and a dedicated legal defense fund designed to shield developers and end-users from licensing fees and patent litigation.

The AOMedia Royalty-Free Patent License

The foundation of AVIF’s patent-free status is the AOMedia Patent License. Founded by technology giants such as Google, Microsoft, Apple, Amazon, Netflix, and Intel, AOMedia mandates that all members contribute their essential patents to the format under a worldwide, non-exclusive, royalty-free, and perpetual license. Because AVIF uses the compression architecture of the AV1 video codec inside the standard HEIF/ISOBMFF container format, any patent owned by AOMedia members that is essential to implementing AVIF is covered by this blanket royalty-free agreement. Anyone—whether an AOMedia member or an independent third party—can create, decode, encode, and distribute AVIF files without paying royalties.

Defensive Termination Clauses

To discourage patent litigation against the format, AOMedia integrates a "defensive termination" clause into its licensing terms. Under this rule, if a company or individual brings a patent infringement lawsuit against any party implementing AVIF or AV1, the plaintiff immediately and automatically loses their own royalty-free license to the technology. This creates a strong legal deterrent against patent aggression, as filing a suit against an AVIF user exposes the plaintiff to severe counter-liabilities and forfeits their right to use the format.

Rigorous Pre-Release Patent Vetting

Rather than developing features without legal oversight, AOMedia implements strict internal patent scrubbing during the codec's design phase. Independent legal counsel and technical patent analysis teams review candidate code and algorithmic tools proposed for the AV1 specification. If a contributed technology carries a high risk of infringing on known third-party patents, the working group deliberately designs around the patent, re-engineers the mechanism, or removes the feature entirely. This proactive approach minimizes exposure to "submarine patents" held by non-participating entities before the standard is finalized.

The Legal Defense and Protection Fund

To further insulate the ecosystem, AOMedia established a defense fund to assist implementers facing patent claims from non-practicing entities or rival standard-setting bodies. Backed by the financial and legal resources of its tier-one members, this fund helps ensure that small developers, open-source projects, and large enterprises can implement AVIF without fearing that patent assertion entities (patent trolls) will target them without legal recourse.

Transparent Open Source Standardization

AVIF specifications and reference implementations (such as libavif) are developed openly. Open-source tracking provides an immutable public record of contributions and provenance, preventing covert introduction of encumbered intellectual property. This open, documented lineage allows adopters to deploy AVIF with legal confidence across web browsers, operating systems, and media platforms.

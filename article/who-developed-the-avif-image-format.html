Who Developed the AVIF Image Format?

The AV1 Image File Format (AVIF) has emerged as one of the most efficient image formats for the modern web, delivering superior compression compared to legacy formats like JPEG and WebP. This article details the organization that spearheaded the development of the AVIF specification, examining how the standard was created, its underlying technology, and its impact on digital media.

The organization responsible for spearheading the AVIF specification is the Alliance for Open Media (AOMedia). Established in 2015, AOMedia is a non-profit consortium formed by major technology companies, including Google, Mozilla, Microsoft, Apple, Netflix, Amazon, Meta, and Intel. The consortium's primary mission is to develop open, royalty-free multimedia technologies to replace proprietary standards that require costly licensing fees.

AOMedia officially released the AVIF 1.0.0 specification in February 2019. The format was developed by taking the advanced intra-frame compression algorithms from AOMedia’s AV1 video codec and encapsulating them within the standardized High Efficiency Image File Format (HEIF) container structure. By leveraging the compression capabilities originally designed for 4K and 8K video streaming, AOMedia created an image format capable of reducing file sizes by up to 50 percent compared to JPEG while preserving high visual fidelity.

Beyond size reduction, AOMedia designed AVIF to handle modern graphical demands. The specification natively supports High Dynamic Range (HDR), 10-bit and 12-bit color depth, wide color gamuts, transparency (alpha channels), and animated image sequences. Because the format is completely open-source and royalty-free, it quickly gained widespread support across all major web browsers, operating systems, and image processing tools, cementing AOMedia's role as a major driving force in modern web performance standards.

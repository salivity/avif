Why Major Platforms Transition from WebP to AVIF

Major web platforms and e-commerce giants are increasingly bypassing or upgrading from WebP directly to AVIF (AV1 Image File Format) to handle large-scale catalog imagery. While WebP served as a significant upgrade over legacy JPEG formats, AVIF delivers substantial improvements in compression efficiency, color fidelity, and visual detail at ultra-low file sizes. For platforms handling millions of product impressions daily, adopting AVIF drastically cuts content delivery network (CDN) bandwidth expenses and improves page loading speeds without sacrificing product presentation quality.

Superior Compression and Bandwidth Savings

At high-volume catalog scale, file size directly impacts both infrastructure costs and bounce rates. AVIF is derived from the AV1 video codec and offers approximately 20% to 30% better compression efficiency than WebP, and up to 50% greater efficiency than standard JPEG. For an enterprise catalog serving billions of monthly image requests, reducing average asset payloads by even a few kilobytes translates to thousands of dollars saved in data transfer and storage costs.

Preserving Product Detail at Low Bitrates

Catalog imagery demands clarity for product textures, stitching, intricate patterns, and typography. When compressed aggressively to meet web performance budgets, WebP often introduces noticeable blurring, edge halos, and loss of fine surface detail. AVIF utilizes advanced directional intra-prediction and transform blocks, allowing it to maintain sharp edges, retain micro-textures, and avoid harsh blocking or banding artifacts even when compressed down to minimal bitrates.

Enhanced Color Fidelity and Modern Display Support

Color accuracy is vital for retail catalogs, as inaccurate representations lead directly to customer dissatisfaction and elevated product return rates. Standard WebP is strictly limited to 8-bit color depth, which frequently leads to color banding in smooth gradients and shadows. In contrast, AVIF supports 10-bit and 12-bit color depths, High Dynamic Range (HDR), and wide color gamuts such as DCI-P3. This ensures that merchandise appears vibrant, accurate, and consistent across modern mobile and desktop screens.

The "Write Once, Serve Millions" Equation

The primary historical drawback of AVIF was its encoding complexity, which requires more CPU cycles than WebP or JPEG. However, catalog architectures operate on an asymmetric delivery model: images are ingested, processed, and encoded only once, but then cached globally and served millions of times. The CPU cost of a single slow encode during catalog upload is entirely negligible compared to the recurring bandwidth and performance gains realized across the image's lifecycle.

Browser Readiness and Industry Alignment

By the time WebP achieved true universal browser adoption—largely delayed until Apple added Safari support in 2020—AVIF was already well into development with backing from the Alliance for Open Media (AOMedia), which includes Google, Apple, Amazon, Microsoft, and Netflix. Because major browsers adopted AVIF much faster than they did WebP, engineering teams running legacy JPEG pipelines realized that migrating directly to AVIF yielded a substantially higher return on investment than pausing at WebP.

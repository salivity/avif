AVIF Royalty-Free Status and Sisvel Patent Pools

The AV1 Image File Format (AVIF) is marketed as an open, royalty-free alternative to proprietary image codecs, promising superior compression and quality without licensing fees. However, patent management firms like Sisvel have formed competing patent pools asserting claims over the underlying AV1 technology. This article examines how Sisvel's claims challenge AVIF's royalty-free status, the legal mechanics behind these patent disputes, and the practical implications for web developers and commercial enterprises adopting the format.

The Foundation of AVIF and AOMedia's Promise

AVIF was developed by the Alliance for Open Media (AOMedia), a consortium of tech giants including Google, Apple, Microsoft, Amazon, and Netflix. AVIF utilizes the intra-frame compression algorithms of the AV1 video standard.

To ensure the format remains free to use, AOMedia established a royalty-free patent license model. Member companies agree to cross-license their essential patents without charging royalties. Furthermore, AOMedia includes a defensive termination clause: if a party sues an AOMedia member or licensee for patent infringement related to AV1, that party forfeits its own rights to the AOMedia patent pool. This architecture is designed to create a legally defended perimeter around AV1 and AVIF.

Enter the Sisvel Patent Pool

Despite AOMedia’s efforts, standard-essential patents (SEPs) present a recurring vulnerability in multimedia standards. In 2019, Sisvel, an intellectual property management company, announced an AV1 patent pool. Sisvel asserts that over a thousand patents owned by third-party innovators—entities that are not members of AOMedia—are essential to practicing the AV1 specification.

Because these outside patent holders never signed the AOMedia royalty-free patent pledge, they are not legally bound by it. Sisvel has structured licensing rates that require device manufacturers, software creators, and specific commercial users to pay per-unit royalties for AV1 decoding and encoding capabilities, directly targeting the foundational technology used by AVIF.

How Patent Pools Threaten Royalty-Free Claims

The presence of the Sisvel pool undermines the absolute claim that AVIF is "royalty-free" in three distinct ways:

  • Third-Party Patent Exposure: AOMedia can only grant licenses to patents owned or controlled by its members. It cannot eliminate the rights of outside patent holders. If Sisvel's member patents are legally valid and technically essential to AVIF, companies using the format could technically be infringing on those patents unless they secure a license.
  • The Distinction Between Specification and Implementation: While the AVIF specification itself is freely available and open source, deploying implementations (such as hardware decoders or commercial encoding software) exposes vendors to liability. Patent pools often target hardware manufacturers and platform providers rather than individual web developers, creating friction at the infrastructure level.
  • Legal Uncertainty and Chilling Effects: For large enterprises, intellectual property ambiguity creates risk. Even if patent claims are disputed, the threat of protracted, costly litigation can slow down the native adoption of AVIF in hardware, operating systems, and commercial software.

AOMedia’s Defense Mechanisms

AOMedia actively resists claims by patent aggregators like Sisvel. The alliance maintains that the AV1 format was engineered to bypass existing proprietary IP or utilize prior art to render outside patent claims invalid.

To protect the ecosystem, AOMedia operates the AOMedia Patent Defense Program. This initiative assists members in defending against patent infringement lawsuits, providing legal resources and identifying prior art to invalidate claims brought by pools like Sisvel. European and American antitrust authorities have also intermittently scrutinized whether such patent pools engage in anti-competitive behavior against open-source standards.

Practical Impact on Everyday Users

For the average web developer, content creator, or small business serving AVIF images on a website, the impact of the Sisvel patent pool remains minimal. Patent pools typically target large hardware vendors, browser makers, and high-volume platform distributors rather than end users. Because the major operating systems and web browsers have integrated AVIF support under their own legal protections, standard web deployment remains broadly shielded.

Nonetheless, Sisvel’s ongoing efforts demonstrate that in modern digital media, "royalty-free" is rarely an absolute technical guarantee. Instead, it is a continuous legal defense supported by the collective market power and legal resources of the format's backing consortium.
